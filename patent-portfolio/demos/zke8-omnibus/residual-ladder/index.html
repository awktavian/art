Seed law. house is this repo's convention: Xorshift128+ over an FNV-1a hash of the seed string. estate is js/seed-stream.js, a bit-exact port of Kagami's own stream — SHA-256 in counter mode over a canonical seed prefix, big-endian 64-bit words, 53-bit draws (packages/kagami/core/omni/prng.py). Both laws fill the SAME eight carrier lanes from the same mapping, so the law is the only thing that changes when you switch it. The estate law is graded, not asserted: tools/gen-estate-seed-vectors.py runs the real Python module, tools/determinism-harness.mjs compares this browser port to its bytes, and tools/browser-check.mjs re-grades the estate frame in Chromium against its own row in tools/pin.json.

What the pixels mean

  • Gold plate — the level-0 codeword is a root class (q=1): 120 of the 256 classes in E8/2E8.
  • Blue plate — a singular class (q=0, non-zero): 135 of 256.
  • Pale plate — the zero class: the carrier landed in the cell whose codeword is 0.
  • Magenta — UNDECLARED: Σw² not divisible by 8, so the point is not in the lattice. Never a palette accident, never folded into the zero class.
  • Brightness — depth inside the cell, against the covering radius sqrt(2).
  • Filament — |advantage| below the drawn radius: the neighbourhood of the set the kernel refuses at 1e-4.

Why this is the estate's codec, not a lattice-flavoured shader

Every pixel samples an eight-lane carrier field v_k(u,v,t) = A · tri(fx_k·u + fy_k·v + phi_k + t·omega_k), whose coefficients come from the seed through the house PRNG (Xorshift128+ on an FNV-1a hash, the same lane as gen/). That vector is then quantized by the residual E8 ladder exactly as ResidualE8LatticeVQ.forward does: divide the residual by the level scale, find the nearest lattice point, subtract, repeat. Level scales are float32(2.0 / sqrt(240)^i) — 15.491933384829668 per level — clamped at 1e-6 at the use site, with the first clamped index measured at 6. The reconstruction is Σ codes[i]/2 · s_i.

What the two paths disagree about, measured

The reference is float64; the shader is float32 from the uniform upload to the fragment's last multiply. Both implement the same expressions — the shader carries the protocol's stop test, the per-level weights, the class hues, the uv law (with the GL Y-flip) and the filament ramp, and it reads its level scales from uScales so the GPU cannot invent a decay. The readout above therefore prints a mean and max per-byte delta and the two digests at load, on this device, instead of asserting the frames match. Where a difference is large enough to see, the worst pixel is named with its coordinates.

The filament radius deserves the same honesty: at the kernel's own 1e-4 the refusal set is 2 of 16384 pixels in the canonical frame (0.01%, min observed |advantage| 3.457e-5). The gold lines draw that set's neighbourhood, and the radius is a named control, not an implied claim.
